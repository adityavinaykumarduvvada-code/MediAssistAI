# Complete Blood Count (CBC) — Plain-Language Reference

A CBC measures the cells that make up blood: red blood cells, white blood
cells, and platelets. It is one of the most common tests doctors order
because it gives a broad snapshot of general health.

## Hemoglobin (Hb)
Hemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Typical
adult reference ranges are roughly 13.5–17.5 g/dL for men and 12.0–15.5
g/dL for women, though exact lab ranges vary by instrument and population.
Low hemoglobin (anemia) can cause fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath,
and dizziness. Common causes include iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or
folate deficiency, chronic disease, or blood loss. High hemoglobin can
relate to dehydration, smoking, lung disease, or living at high altitude.

## White Blood Cells (WBC)
White blood cells are part of the immune system and fight infection.
A typical adult reference range is about 4,000–11,000 cells/µL. An
elevated WBC count (leukocytosis) often suggests infection, inflammation,
or stress on the body, and sometimes points to more serious conditions
that need follow-up. A low WBC count (leukopenia) can result from viral
infections, certain medications, or bone marrow problems.

## Platelets
Platelets help blood clot. A typical reference range is about
150,000–450,000/µL. Low platelet counts (thrombocytopenia) can increase
bruising or bleeding risk. High platelet counts can occur with
inflammation, iron deficiency, or after surgery.

## When to see a doctor
Any single abnormal value should be interpreted alongside symptoms and
other tests by a qualified clinician — reference ranges vary between labs,
and one abnormal number rarely tells the whole story on its own.
