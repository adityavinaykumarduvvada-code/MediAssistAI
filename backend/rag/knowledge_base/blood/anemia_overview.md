# Anemia — Plain-Language Reference

Anemia means the blood has fewer healthy red blood cells or less
hemoglobin than normal, so tissues receive less oxygen. It is a finding,
not a single disease — many different underlying causes can lead to it.

## Common types and causes
- **Iron-deficiency anemia**: the most common global type, often from
  low dietary iron, poor absorption, or chronic blood loss (e.g. heavy
  periods, gastrointestinal bleeding).
- **Vitamin deficiency anemia**: low vitamin B12 or folate impairs red
  blood cell production.
- **Anemia of chronic disease**: long-term conditions such as kidney
  disease, cancer, or chronic inflammation can suppress red cell
  production.
- **Hemolytic anemia**: red blood cells are destroyed faster than they
  are made.

## Typical symptoms
Fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath on exertion, lightheadedness,
cold hands and feet, and a fast or irregular heartbeat in more severe
cases.

## Typical work-up
Doctors usually pair a CBC with additional tests — such as ferritin,
iron studies, vitamin B12/folate levels, or a reticulocyte count — to
identify the underlying cause before deciding on treatment, which may
range from dietary changes and supplements to treating an underlying
condition.
