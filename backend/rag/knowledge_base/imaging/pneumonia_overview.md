# Pneumonia — Plain-Language Reference

Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs,
which can fill with fluid or pus. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses,
or fungi. Community guidelines (broadly consistent with WHO and national
respiratory society recommendations) describe common symptoms as cough
(often with phlegm), fever, chills, difficulty breathing, chest pain that
worsens with breathing or coughing, and fatigue.

## Chest X-ray correlation
On a chest X-ray, pneumonia often appears as an area of increased density
or "opacity" — a whitish, cloudy patch where the air-filled lung normally
looks dark. Radiologists describe patterns such as lobar consolidation
(affecting one section of a lung), patchy/interstitial patterns, or
bilateral involvement. Opacity alone is not a confirmed diagnosis — it can
also reflect fluid buildup, scarring, or other conditions, so clinical
correlation (symptoms, listening to the lungs, blood tests, and sometimes
a CT scan) is standard practice.

## General management principles
Treatment depends on the suspected cause and severity. Bacterial pneumonia
is typically treated with antibiotics selected by a clinician based on
local resistance patterns, patient history, and severity. Supportive care
(rest, fluids, fever control) is common for milder viral cases. Warning
signs that usually prompt urgent care include difficulty breathing,
bluish lips or face, persistent high fever, confusion, or low oxygen
saturation.
