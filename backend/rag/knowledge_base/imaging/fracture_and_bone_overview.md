# Bone X-rays & Fractures — Plain-Language Reference

X-rays are the first-line imaging test for suspected bone injuries
because bone absorbs X-ray radiation strongly and appears bright white
against darker soft tissue.

## What a fracture looks like on X-ray
A fracture typically appears as a visible break or discontinuity in the
normally smooth white line of the bone. Radiologists describe fractures
by pattern (transverse, oblique, spiral, comminuted), displacement (how
far the pieces have shifted), and whether the break reaches a joint
surface. Hairline (stress) fractures can be subtle and are sometimes
missed on an initial X-ray, requiring follow-up imaging like a CT or MRI
if pain persists despite a "normal" X-ray.

## General management principles
Treatment depends on the bone involved, the fracture pattern, and
displacement — ranging from a simple cast or splint to surgical
fixation with plates, screws, or rods for unstable or displaced
fractures. Healing time varies widely by age, bone, and overall health,
commonly weeks to a few months.
