# Pain & Inflammation Medicines — Plain-Language Reference

## Ibuprofen
A nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used for pain, fever, and
inflammation (e.g. sprains, arthritis, menstrual pain). Common side
effects include stomach upset; taking it with food helps. Long-term or
high-dose use carries risk of stomach ulcers and kidney strain, and it
should be used cautiously alongside blood thinners or in people with
kidney disease.

## Diclofenac
Another NSAID, often prescribed as tablets, gels, or patches for joint
and muscle pain. Similar cautions to ibuprofen regarding stomach and
kidney effects with prolonged use.

## Tramadol
A prescription-only opioid-like pain medication used for moderate to
moderately severe pain when NSAIDs or paracetamol aren't enough. Can
cause drowsiness, nausea, and dependence with prolonged use; should not
be combined with alcohol or certain antidepressants without medical
guidance.
