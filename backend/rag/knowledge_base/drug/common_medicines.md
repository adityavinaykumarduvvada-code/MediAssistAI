# Common Medicines — Plain-Language Reference

## Paracetamol / Acetaminophen
Used to relieve mild-to-moderate pain and reduce fever. Considered
generally safe at recommended doses, but exceeding the maximum daily
dose can cause serious liver damage, especially when combined with
alcohol or other paracetamol-containing products. Common brand names
include Tylenol, Crocin, and Calpol.

## Amoxicillin
A penicillin-type antibiotic used for many bacterial infections,
including some respiratory, ear, and urinary tract infections. Common
side effects include nausea, diarrhea, and rash. It should not be used
by people with a penicillin allergy. Antibiotics should always be
finished as prescribed even if symptoms improve early, and are not
effective against viral infections.

## Azithromycin
A macrolide antibiotic often prescribed for respiratory infections
(including some pneumonia cases), sinusitis, and certain STIs. Typically
given as a short course. Possible side effects include stomach upset and,
rarely, heart rhythm changes in people with existing cardiac risk
factors.

## Metformin
A first-line oral medication for type 2 diabetes that helps lower blood
sugar by reducing glucose production in the liver and improving insulin
sensitivity. Common side effects include gastrointestinal upset,
especially when starting treatment; taking it with food can help.

## Atorvastatin
A statin medication used to lower LDL ("bad") cholesterol and reduce
cardiovascular risk. Possible side effects include muscle aches and,
rarely, liver enzyme changes, so periodic monitoring may be recommended.

## Cetirizine
An antihistamine used to relieve allergy symptoms such as sneezing,
runny nose, and itching. Generally causes less drowsiness than older
antihistamines, though some people still experience mild sedation.

## Omeprazole
A proton pump inhibitor that reduces stomach acid production, commonly
used for acid reflux, heartburn, and stomach ulcers. Long-term use is
usually reviewed periodically with a doctor.
